# The thirteen-job map

**Scope:** FieldWorks 9 family; FLEx 9.3 WinForms terminology baseline from the September design.
Help branch: `sillsdev/FwHelps:markdown-export`, no release tag verified here.
Model vocabulary: `SIL.LCModel 11.0.0-beta0182`. This reference promises no current screen layout.

A documenting linguist needs usable records and explanations throughout the work, rather than only a
parser configuration. This map is an ordering aid; fieldwork revisits earlier jobs as evidence changes.

The areas below orient the need; they are not a click-path or assertion about visible controls.

| Job | Intended result | FLEx area / external boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Setup and orthography | Agreed writing systems and interpretable forms | Project setup; consult the FieldWorks expert |
| 2. Elicitation | Forms, judgments and provenance that test a question | Texts / Research Notebook; recording happens outside FLEx too |
| 3. Word collection | Entries linked to sources, senses and variants | Lexicon |
| 4. Interlinearization | Segmentation, glosses and translations kept distinct | Texts & Words |
| 5. Morphology | Paradigms and licensed readings; affixes, MSAs, slots, templates | Grammar and Lexicon; parser expert supplies mechanics |
| 6. Phonology | Segment inventory and conditioned alternations | Grammar; parser expert checks reach |
| 7. Cross-checking against text | Known readings reproduced, negatives excluded, incompletion visible | Texts & Words; parser evidence |
| 8. Discourse | Interpretations in connected speech and context | Texts & Words; specialized analysis may be external |
| 9. Notebook | Hypotheses, contradictions, sources and open questions | Research Notebook |
| 10. Sketch | A readable grammatical account consistent with evidence | Grammar sketch and external writing |
| 11. Publication | Useful dictionary or text materials for intended readers | Lexicon publication and external publishing |
| 12. Collaboration | Shared decisions and accountable review | Project collaboration; authority remains with people |
| 13. Archiving | Recoverable records, provenance and community access | Export and external archival arrangements |

## Five tensions to preserve

- Texts-first versus paradigm-first: texts expose usage; paradigms discriminate a specific contrast.
  Choose for the current question, then check the other source of evidence.
- Lexicon-driven versus grammar-driven: record observed exceptions without losing a generalization.
  Revisit the division when more stems are available.
- Depth versus breadth: a thoroughly checked paradigm and wide lexical coverage measure different needs.
  Report the exercised scope rather than suggesting one proves the other.
- Two meanings of “same analysis”: morphology equivalence is a bounded parser comparison; a speaker’s
  sense or contextual interpretation can distinguish readings with the same morphology.
- Who judges “better”: parsers settle reproducible engine facts, people settle linguistic preferences,
  and the user’s job determines whether a proposed explanation or workflow helps.

This synthesis follows the September design. Bowern’s contents and Moe’s introduction support its
breadth; they do not prescribe this exact order or these tension labels. See the local bibliography.
