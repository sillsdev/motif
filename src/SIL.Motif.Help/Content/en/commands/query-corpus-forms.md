# Query corpus forms

`motif query --corpus <words.tsv|folder> forms where <expression>` (also `forms --where <expression>` or `forms --query-json <file|->`) lists the words of a plain word list that satisfy a query expression, with no project, Trial or parse. The corpus is read as for `query pairs`; the expression is the text form of `motif.query.v2`, over the columns `form`, `tokens`, `parse.state`, `shape`, `syllables`, `fold`, `initial:n` and `final:n`.

```powershell
motif query --corpus words.tsv forms where 'phon(Stop V Stop V)' --grammar project.fwdata
motif query --corpus texts/ forms --where 'env([V] / # _ Stop) and tokens > 2' --grammar project.fwdata --json
```

`phon` and `env` need `--grammar <path>`, the grammar file `pangloss serve` loads; PanGloss segments the words with its surface character table and names the natural classes. Without it they are refused with `query.phon-needs-grammar`. Rows come by token count then form, `--limit` (default 100) keeps the first; a cut says `TRUNCATED`. The header counts the words the grammar cannot segment and lists a sample; `--json` writes `motif.query.corpus-forms.v1` with each row's `matches`. See `query forms` for the pattern language.

`realizes(<morpheme>, attested)` finds the words that carry a morpheme in any surface form the grammar gives it. With `--grammar <path>`, Motif parses a sample of the corpus's word types with the resident PanGloss, keeps the analyses that contain the morpheme, and takes the morpheme's surface piece from the shape PanGloss reports for each analysis; where the shape does not line up with the word, the whole word stands in and the header says `piece_unknown`. A parse costs about 0.4 to 1.7 s a word on a large grammar, so `--sample <n>` (default 300, `0` for every type) takes a frequency-weighted sample that is the same each run; the forms it finds are then searched for across the whole corpus. With `--project <fwdata>`, Motif also reads that project's stored analyses (never writing it), and the forms from both sources are joined and labelled. The morpheme is `entry:msa` or an id, which need no project; a form such as `sfx:-an` (optionally led by `root:`, `pfx:` or `sfx:`) needs `--project` to name the entry.

The forms compile to one search over the words' surface text, anchored at the start for a prefix and the end for a suffix. The header says what was searched for, whether the set is complete, how many words were parsed, and that the search can over-match, because it reads characters and not the grammar's segments; each row names the forms it carries. A sample, a word the grammar cannot segment, a parse stopped at a limit and a missing surface piece each make the set not complete, so `not realizes(...)` is refused as `query.realizes-negation-incomplete` until every word has been parsed (`--sample 0`) and answered. A morpheme found in no parsed word is refused as `query.realizes-no-parses`. The tiers `possible` and `generated` parse and are refused as not yet available.
