# Query corpus forms

`motif query --corpus <words.tsv|folder> forms where <expression>` (also `forms --where <expression>` or `forms --query-json <file|->`) lists the words of a plain word list that satisfy a query expression, with no project, Trial or parse. The corpus is read as for `query pairs`; the expression is the text form of `motif.query.v2`, over the columns `form`, `tokens`, `parse.state`, `shape`, `syllables`, `fold`, `initial:n` and `final:n`.

```powershell
motif query --corpus words.tsv forms where 'phon(Stop V Stop V)' --grammar project.fwdata
motif query --corpus texts/ forms --where 'env([V] / # _ Stop) and tokens > 2' --grammar project.fwdata --json
```

`phon` and `env` need `--grammar <path>`, the grammar file `pangloss serve` loads; PanGloss segments the words with its surface character table and names the natural classes. Without it they are refused with `query.phon-needs-grammar`. Rows come by token count then form, `--limit` (default 100) keeps the first; a cut says `TRUNCATED`. The header counts the words the grammar cannot segment and lists a sample; `--json` writes `motif.query.corpus-forms.v1` with each row's `matches`. See `query forms` for the pattern language.

`realizes(<morpheme>, attested)` finds the words that carry a morpheme in any surface form the grammar gives it. With `--grammar <path>`, PanGloss parses every word type of the corpus once with `batch --analyses --morph-spans` (PanGloss v0.34.0 or later; an older one is refused as `query.realizes-index-failed` with `needs-pangloss-v0.34.0`) and Motif keeps the result as a parse index under its cache folder (`SIL/Motif/cache/realizes-parse-index` in the user data folder), keyed by the grammar file, the corpus's word types and the PanGloss build; the first query writes "building parse index" to standard error and takes about a minute and a half on a large grammar, and later queries load the index in a few seconds. The index records, for each analysis, each morph's ids and the surface text PanGloss places it at, so a matching word gives the morpheme's own piece with nothing traced. A morph PanGloss could not place (a typed null such as `span_lost_by_rule`, `span_ambiguous_copy` or `compound_unsupported`), or whose spans do not add up to the word's surface, stands in as its whole word and the header says `piece_unplaced` or `piece_inconsistent`. With `--project <fwdata>`, Motif also reads that project's stored analyses (never writing it), and the forms from both sources are joined and labelled. The morpheme is `entry:msa` or one id (an analysis or form id), which need no project; a form such as `sfx:-an` (optionally led by `root:`, `pfx:` or `sfx:`) needs `--project` to name the entry.

The forms compile to one search over the words' surface text, anchored at the start for a prefix and the end for a suffix. The header says what was searched for, whether the set is complete, how many word types the parse index answered, how many pieces came from spans and how many words stand in whole, and that the search can over-match, because it reads characters and not the grammar's segments; each row names the forms it carries. A word the grammar cannot segment, a parse stopped at a limit, a word the batch gave no answer for and a missing or untrusted surface piece each make the set not complete, so `not realizes(...)` is refused as `query.realizes-negation-incomplete`. If PanGloss fails to parse the corpus, or what it wrote is malformed, the query is refused as `query.realizes-index-failed` with the error output or the line number. An unreadable cached index is deleted and rebuilt once, with a note on standard error, and the cache keeps the eight newest indexes. A morpheme found in no parsed word type is refused as `query.realizes-no-parses`. The tiers `possible` and `generated` parse and are refused as not yet available.

## Readings, causes and retirement

With `--grammar <path>` the corpus is parsed once (the same cached parse index as `realizes`) and each word type gets its readings. Three things read them.

- `readings` is a number column: `forms where 'readings > 3'`. `parse.state` then comes from the parse, not the word list: `parsed`, `none`, `capped`, `timed-out`, `unsegmentable` or `failed`. Rows show the reading count.
- `--group-by cause` groups the matching words that have more than one reading by why, and returns `{groups:[{cause, benign, words, tokens, example:{word, readings:[{morphs}]}}]}`. In priority order the causes are `lexicalised-vs-derived` (one reading has a single stem over a span another covers with a root plus derivational affixes), `same-morphs-reordered` (the same morphs in another order), `homophonous-affix` (readings differ only in affixes that spell the same but have different analyses, such as SM and OM; `benign:true`), and `other`. A word gets the highest-priority cause any pair of its readings shows; it is homophonous only when every differing pair is. With `--project <fwdata>` (or a `.fwdata` as `--grammar`) each morph carries its entry and class, so derivational affixes are told from inflectional ones; without it the spans alone decide `lexicalised-vs-derived`.
- `only-via(entry:<guid>)` (JSON `{"only-via": {"entry": "<guid>"}}`) matches words that have a reading and whose every reading uses the morpheme; `msa:` and `form:` name an analysis or a form id. PanGloss's batch gives each morph its form and analysis ids but not its entry; an analysis belongs to exactly one entry, so `entry:` needs `--project` (or a `.fwdata` as `--grammar`) and is refused with `query.readings-needs-project` otherwise.
- `--what-if-retire <id>[,<id>...]` (ids as `entry:`, `msa:`, `form:` or a bare id) lists, for the matching words, `readings`, `readingsVia`, `readingsOther`, `lost` (every reading goes) and `reduced` (some stay), with type and token totals. It is an estimate read from the cached reading sets and is labelled `estimate: true`: confirm with a Trial. A word whose parse was capped is flagged, because a hidden reading may remain.

A word whose parse was capped, timed out or got no answer may have more readings than the grammar gives, so the header says `Readings: INCOMPLETE` and the JSON `readingsInfo.complete` is false; the counts are then lower bounds. `--group-by` and `--what-if-retire` need no `where`; the rows are left out and the groups or the estimate are the answer.
