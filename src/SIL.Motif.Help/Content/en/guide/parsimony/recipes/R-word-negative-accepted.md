# A reviewed negative parses

A reviewed negative that parses is direct evidence of a possible restriction problem. The repair must target the witnessed structure and preserve valid readings.

## Analyse

Read the negative judgment, its provenance, the parser's returned morphs, and the current slot order. Check positive forms and alternate readings.

## Disposition

An applied keep or defer is tied to this finding's exact evidence. See [Disposition](term:disposition) for how it appears in Active and Suppressed lists and returns after relevant evidence changes.

Fix a confirmed order error; correct mistaken evidence separately; ask about register or reading differences; defer an unsupported cause.

## Ask the linguist

Ask whether the reversed order is impossible in this category or valid in another use. Show the actual analyses and a matching positive.

## Update

Edit the existing template order for the confirmed category. Keep the reviewed-negative judgment unchanged.

## Verify

Require a completed search with no target reading, preserved Approved readings, and stable held-out controls. If `reviewed-negative-expectations` is unavailable, name it and defer.

[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(67)91165-5). Distinguishes positive-only text from an informant that labels membership and nonmembership.
- **[bender-poulson-drellishak-evans-2007]** — Emily M. Bender, Laurie Poulson, Scott Drellishak, and Chris Evans. 2007. “Validation and Regression Testing for a Cross-linguistic Grammar Resource.” In *ACL 2007 Workshop on Deep Linguistic Processing*, 136–143. Association for Computational Linguistics. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/W07-1218/). Supports keeping positive recovery and negative rejection as separate regression outcomes.
- **[prince-tesar-2004]** — Alan Prince and Bruce Tesar. 2004. “Learning Phonotactic Distributions.” In *Constraints in Phonological Acquisition*, 245–291. Cambridge University Press. DOI: 10.1017/CBO9780511486418.009. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511486418.009). Explains that grammars consistent with positive data can differ on unseen forms, while negative judgments are distinct evidence.

An absent word or an accepted parser probe alone is not a negative judgment.
