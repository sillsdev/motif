# Repeated ad hoc restrictions

Several prohibitions may encode the same parser restriction. Keep one supported statement and verify that the bounded cleanup preserves readings.

## Analyse

Compare the full ordered targets, active load facts, descriptions, and witness cases. Choose the retained identity explicitly.

## Disposition

An applied keep or defer is tied to this finding's exact evidence. See [Disposition](term:disposition) for how it appears in Active and Suppressed lists and returns after relevant evidence changes.

Fix only when the active signatures are identical and useful rationale remains. Keep, ask, or defer when group loading or target expansion is incomplete.

## Ask the linguist

Ask which wording should remain and whether both statements carry different linguistic explanations.

## Update

Disable only the confirmed duplicate. Do not delete either object or rewrite its target list.

## Verify

Confirm the duplicate count falls, the retained rule remains loaded, and completed parse readings stay equal. If `grouped-adhoc-load` is unavailable, name it and defer.

## Grounding

- **[black-2018]** — H. Andrew Black. 2018. “Introduction to Parsing.” SIL FieldWorks technical document, 12 April 2018. [Technical documents index](https://software.sil.org/fieldworks/help/technical-documents/). Supports treating ad hoc prohibitions as a fallback and preserving their ordered, conjunctive targets.
- **[bender-poulson-drellishak-evans-2007]** — Emily M. Bender, Laurie Poulson, Scott Drellishak, and Chris Evans. 2007. “Validation and Regression Testing for a Cross-linguistic Grammar Resource.” In *ACL 2007 Workshop on Deep Linguistic Processing*, 136–143. Association for Computational Linguistics. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/W07-1218/). Supports checking grammar outcomes with regression examples after a proposed cleanup.

The exact-signature trigger is an engineering lint; it does not establish that two authored prohibitions have identical parser effects.
