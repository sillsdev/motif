# Teach a dumb computer your language

You know the words and patterns of your language. A parser does not. It follows the lexicon and rules someone entered for it, one step at a time. An AI consultant may help you work out which parser setting to inspect; Motif gives both of you evidence about what happened.

The work is a loop:

1. **Measure.** Save your FieldWorks work, then choose **Refresh** or choose [Parse all words](term:parse-all-words) to measure words you care about. [Text Coverage](term:text-coverage) tells you how much of the chosen material parsed.
2. **Understand.** Try a difficult word in **Try a Word**. Read the parser’s furthest attempt, then follow its steps to see what it built and where it stopped. Use **Timing** when words take a long time.
3. **Fix.** Use your language knowledge to check the likely cause. Make any grammar or lexicon change in FieldWorks, save it, and measure again.

Motif's synthetic samples are generated to demonstrate its tools and give us small, safe places to practise. They are synthetic-turkic (Turkish-style), synthetic-bantu (Swahili-style), and synthetic-philippine (Tagalog-style), each modelled loosely on the named language. Their data are invented: they are not real language data and must not be used as descriptions of Turkish, Swahili, Tagalog, or any language.

You do not need to become a parser engineer to take part. You can tell an AI consultant what a word should mean and how it is built. Ask it to connect that analysis to the trace, suggest one small change, and name other words that should test the idea. Then check the suggestion against your language knowledge and Motif’s evidence. The AI can help with the machinery; it cannot decide what is right in your language.
