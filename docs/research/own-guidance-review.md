# Grammar-authoring guidance review

The current guidance has a strong technical reference layer, but a linguist still has to cross several documents to move from a language fact to a FieldWorks model and then to a result Motif can actually parse. This review maps that coverage, checks its claims against the checked-in evidence and published FieldWorks guidance, and calls out the boundaries that should remain explicit.

## Scope and evidence

I reviewed the parser-help reference tree, the Learn guide, the grammar projection and coverage documents, the grammar breadth and proper-analysis research notes, the synthetic Bantu, Philippine, and Turkic notes, the Tagalog feasibility sources, and the local read-only FieldWorks help checkout. The parser-help directory says its topics are grounded in HermitCrab/FieldWorks source and that it excludes real project data (`docs/parser-help/README.md:3-9,53-59`). I also checked published SIL FieldWorks grammar and parser help, including the [Grammar feature page](https://software.sil.org/fieldworks/features/orientation-to-fieldworks/grammar/) (“Wordsets” and “Distinctives of Current Tool”) and the [HermitCrab parsing help](https://downloads.languagetechnology.org/fieldworks/Documentation/en/User_Interface/Menus/Parser/Parsing_words_%28HermitCrab%29.htm) (“Important”).

Keep three scopes distinct throughout: FieldWorks authoring, HermitCrab engine behavior, and the narrower HCLoader/PanGloss implementation. The surface matrix explicitly marks constructs that stop at HCLoader or are not implemented in PanGloss (`docs/hc-surface-scope.md:30-52`). An upstream FieldWorks example establishes how to author that FieldWorks construct; by itself it does not establish that PanGloss imports or runs it.

## Five findings

1. **The technical source base is substantial and unusually traceable.** The parser-help tree indexes 16 broken-grammar notes, 10 performance notes, eight workflow notes, and five text-format notes, with engine-source grounding (`docs/parser-help/README.md:18-35`). The Learn pages are short introductions; the deeper facts are split among the reference pages, model map, surface matrix, and research notes.
2. **The Learn page makes an unsupported general claim about empty affixes and work.** Its wording says empty affixes add paths and may force many combinations to be checked (`help/en/guide/learn/why-a-grammar-is-slow.md:3-9`). The supplied PanGloss measurement is a counterexample for one tested grammar; the page should describe a possible, implementation-dependent cost and point readers to a measured Assessment.
3. **Infix support and reduplication support have different PanGloss outcomes.** The checked-in feasibility test exercises `-um-` and `-in-` infixes, while its reduplication test is skipped with PanGloss v0.5.0's importer limitation (`tests/SIL.Motif.Tests.LibLcm/Samples/TagalogFeasibilityTests.cs:28-39`; `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-26-tagalog-spike-findings.md:15-36`). FLEx documentation of a pattern is not evidence of PanGloss support.
4. **The claim that Black gives no way to find overgeneration is too broad if read as a claim about FieldWorks.** The cited research note says the conceptual guide leaves noticing to reading (`docs/research/2026-08-08-grammar-breadth-black-2025-and-a-path.md:108-120`), but SIL's Grammar page documents wordsets for checking prediction accuracy, and the FieldWorks 9.2.9 release notes describe detailed parser test reports ([Grammar, “Wordsets”](https://software.sil.org/fieldworks/features/orientation-to-fieldworks/grammar/); [FieldWorks 9.2.9, “Automatic parsers”](https://software.sil.org/fieldworks/fieldworks-9-2-9-stable-version/)). Narrow the claim to the methodology in the conceptual guide itself.
5. **The current map is better at describing implementation than teaching authoring.** It names the LibLCM write surface and silent-loss cases (`docs/hc-grammar-map.md:13-27,57-70`), while the Learn guide introduces concepts and the parser-help workflow pages explain decisions. There is no single, end-to-end linguist workflow connecting a construct, its authoring fields, importer support, expected diagnostics, and a measured result.

## Contradictions and qualifications

| Claim pair | Finding |
|---|---|
| Empty affixes necessarily add PanGloss search work | The Learn page says empty affixes add paths (`help/en/guide/learn/why-a-grammar-is-slow.md:3-9`). The Bantu research note labels its optional-slot/zero-affix example a search-space prediction, not a measured PanGloss result (`docs/research/synthetic-samples/bantu.md:148`). The task brief supplies a measurement in which 160 empty object-affix allomorphs leave work at 1,723 and 158 steps, while duplicating real `m-`/`wa-` affixes raises work 19.5×. I did not find a checked-in benchmark output or source note for those exact figures. Treat them as brief-supplied evidence pending an artifact; they refute a universal claim if reproducible, but do not prove that empty forms never cost work in other grammars. Black's warning is about the FieldWorks parser and should remain scoped to that implementation (Black extract §4.1.1, `docs/parser-help/workflow/sources/black-flex-conceptual-intro-fulltext.txt:4076-4128`). |
| Black's guide offers no way to detect overgeneration | The 2026 research note correctly observes that the conceptual guide does not provide a systematic assessment protocol (`docs/research/2026-08-08-grammar-breadth-black-2025-and-a-path.md:108-120`). Read as a statement about FieldWorks tools, however, it is contradicted by SIL's documented wordsets accuracy checking and parser test reports ([Grammar, “Wordsets”](https://software.sil.org/fieldworks/features/orientation-to-fieldworks/grammar/); [FieldWorks 9.2.9, “Automatic parsers”](https://software.sil.org/fieldworks/fieldworks-9-2-9-stable-version/)). Revise the research wording to separate Black's pedagogical method from the product's available evaluation tools. |
| Invalid environments are rejected vs. malformed strings widen silently | The map reports that an invalid environment string passed to HCLoader can become “applies everywhere” (`docs/hc-grammar-map.md:31-34,57-64`). FieldWorks user help describes editor validation and parser-facing behavior for malformed environments ([Insert an environment](https://downloads.languagetechnology.org/fieldworks/Documentation/en/Using_Tools/Grammar_tools/Environments/Insert_an_environment.htm)). These statements may describe different points in the authoring/import pipeline, so the review cannot resolve them as a direct contradiction. Add a pinned fixture for malformed persisted `StringRepresentation.Text` and state the boundary. |
| FLEx reduplication examples vs. PanGloss importer | These are different support layers, not inconsistent facts: FLEx teaches the indexed reduplication notation, while the repository records PanGloss v0.5.0 warnings and a skipped parser test (`docs/hc-surface-scope.md:45-48`; `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-26-tagalog-spike-findings.md:27-39`). Keep both claims with their engine/version labels. |

## Claims to mark unverified

- **Bantu empty-affix and real-affix benchmark figures:** supplied in the task brief but not traceable to a checked-in run artifact in the reviewed source set. Add the input fixture, command/version, and work-count output before treating the figures as independently reproducible.
- **PanGloss performance implications of broad environments or optional slots:** the technical notes explain HermitCrab mechanisms, and the Bantu/Philippine sample notes identify modeled search paths, but no general PanGloss measurement was found. The Philippine note explicitly calls its branching count a prediction, not a measured slowdown (`docs/research/synthetic-samples/philippine.md:94-104`).
- **Current source edition of the Black guide:** the local full-text extraction supports citations by section and page, but the review did not confirm a stable public link for the exact extracted edition. Keep the local source citation and edition provenance visible; do not imply the 2018 public URL and the local FLEx 9 extract are the same publication.
- **Malformed-environment fallback as an end-to-end user outcome:** the model map describes the HCLoader failure mode, but the reviewed evidence does not establish whether ordinary FieldWorks UI validation prevents every affected project from reaching that path.
- **Every HCLoader/PanGloss matrix check as an end-to-end acceptance test:** the matrix labels several constructs partial or untested, including stem names and environment grammars (`docs/hc-surface-scope.md:32-52`). Preserve its `~`, `✗`, and untested distinctions; do not flatten them into “supported.”

## Open questions and recommended follow-through

1. Where is the reproducible benchmark artifact for the 160-empty-affix and 19.5× real-affix comparisons? Record its synthetic grammar, PanGloss version, work metric, and rerun command.
2. Should the empty-affix Learn page describe a general HermitCrab possibility, a PanGloss observation, or both in separate sentences? The current wording merges them.
3. Can a focused fixture confirm the importer behavior for malformed persisted environment strings and reconcile it with FieldWorks editor validation?
4. What is the exact edition/provenance of the checked-in Black full-text extraction, and can its citation point to a stable public document?
5. Which diagnostics should a linguist see when an upstream FieldWorks construct is stored successfully but is skipped or widened by HCLoader/PanGloss?

The practical next step is to correct the two overbroad statements (empty-affix cost and Black's lack of evaluation tools), add explicit version/layer labels to examples that cross from FieldWorks into PanGloss, and keep the construct matrix as the reference boundary for what is tested, partial, and not yet imported.
