# Synthetic Turkic-style sample: Turkish morphology research

> **SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE.** This language data was generated to demonstrate Motif. It is modelled loosely on Turkish, but it is not real Turkish, has not been checked by speakers, and must not be used as a description of any language. This is a research draft for a teaching project, not a Turkish grammar.

## 1. Plain-language overview

Turkish nouns can add endings for plural number, a possessor, and case, in that order. The endings change their vowels to fit the most recent vowel in the word: some choose between **a/e**, while others choose among **ı/i/u/ü**. A consonant may also appear between two vowels, and the locative and ablative endings begin with **t** after a voiceless consonant but **d** elsewhere. Those regular contrasts make a compact way to teach why a parser needs both ordered suffix slots and narrowly conditioned allomorphs. The proposed project borrows only a small, regular subset of this system. [G1, §§5.1, 5.3, 6.2–6.5; G2, Appendix]

## 2. Analysis needed by the sample

### Scope and FieldWorks objects

Author the teaching lexicon as **Noun** stems with an English gloss; use one active Noun inflectional affix template, `STEM – (PL) – (POSS.1SG) – (CASE)`. Parentheses mean an optional slot, and the case slot admits **at most one** case affix. A bare stem has no overt singular, possessive, or nominative suffix. The three slots are optional in the *template*, but their order is obligatory whenever more than one is filled. The sample can include `ev-ler-im-de` (house-PL-POSS.1SG-LOC); it must reject a reordering such as `ev-im-ler-de` as an analysis with the same three inflectional functions. Turkish's wider noun paradigm has other possessors and cases, but this is the complete proposed teaching subset. [G1, §§6.2, 6.4–6.5; G2, “Noun Suffixes Examined”; F1]

Create phonological natural classes in Grammar for `Front = {e,i,ö,ü}`, `Back = {a,ı,o,u}`, `I→i = {e,i}`, `I→ı = {a,ı}`, `I→ü = {ö,ü}`, `I→u = {o,u}`, `Vowel = {a,e,ı,i,o,ö,u,ü}`, and `Voiceless final = {ç,f,h,k,p,s,ş,t}`. The four `I→…` classes group **preceding vowels** by which suffix vowel they select; they are not classes of high vowels. FieldWorks can use natural classes in allomorph environments; where a final consonant intervenes, constrain by the **last vowel**, not simply the immediately preceding segment. A project may express this with separate environments for vowel-final and single-coda-consonant stems, or with phonological features/rules. Do not put an unconditioned allomorph before conditioned alternatives: FieldWorks tests allomorphs in order and later choices inherit exclusions from earlier environments. [G1, §§5.1, 5.3; G2, Appendix; F2; F3]

| Morpheme and category | Allomorphs in this subset | Conditioning at the attachment point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PL`, noun number | `-ler`, `-lar` | `-ler` after the last **front** vowel; `-lar` after the last **back** vowel. Optional plural slot. [G1, §6.3; G2, Appendix] |
| `POSS.1SG`, noun possession | `-im/-ım/-üm/-um` after a consonant; `-m` after a vowel | The vowel of the consonant-final form follows four-way harmony. After a vowel-final host, omit that high vowel. Optional possessive slot. [G1, §6.4; G2, Appendix] |
| `ACC`, noun case | `-i/-ı/-ü/-u` after a consonant; `-yi/-yı/-yü/-yu` after a vowel | Four-way harmony; **y** separates the host vowel from the suffix vowel. Use for a definite direct object, not as an English-style definite article. [G1, §6.5.4; G2, Appendix] |
| `DAT`, noun case | `-e/-a` after a consonant; `-ye/-ya` after a vowel | Two-way harmony; **y** separates adjacent vowels. Direction or dative-governed argument. [G1, §6.5.3; G2, Appendix] |
| `LOC`, noun case | `-de/-da/-te/-ta` | Two-way harmony chooses **e/a**; initial **t** follows a final voiceless consonant, and **d** follows a vowel or voiced consonant. [G1, §§5.3.2, 6.5.5; G2, Appendix] |
| `ABL`, noun case | `-den/-dan/-ten/-tan` | The same vowel and **d/t** conditions as LOC. [G1, §§5.3.2, 6.5.6; G2, Appendix] |
| `GEN`, noun case | `-in/-ın/-ün/-un` after a consonant; `-nin/-nın/-nün/-nun` after a vowel | Four-way harmony; **n** separates adjacent vowels. Use as the possessor's case marker. [G1, §6.5.2; G2, Appendix] |

The suffix notation `A` means **e** after a front vowel and **a** after a back vowel. `I` means **i** after `e/i`, **ı** after `a/ı`, **ü** after `ö/ü`, and **u** after `o/u`. Each suffix sees the **last vowel of the form already built**, including an earlier suffix vowel: `göz-üm` but `göz-ler-im`, and `kol-um` but `kol-lar-ım`. This is why selecting every allomorph from the unsuffixed stem would be wrong. [G1, §§5.1, 6.2–6.4; G2, Appendix; G3, ch. 2]

For the builder, prefer explicitly listed affix allomorphs plus their environments, because the sample's purpose is to make allomorph selection visible in FieldWorks. The underlying phonological generalizations are (1) propagate front/back quality to an `A` or `I` suffix vowel, (2) propagate rounding to `I`, (3) realize `y` before vowel-initial DAT/ACC and `n` before vowel-initial GEN after a vowel-final host, and (4) realize the LOC/ABL initial as `t` after a final voiceless consonant. Apply these left to right **after each suffix is attached**, so the next suffix sees the output of the previous one; buffer selection and the d/t choice inspect the already built host. No additional phonological rule order is needed if the allomorphs encode these choices directly. There is **no infix position environment and no reduplication pattern** in this noun-only sample. [G1, §§5.1, 5.3, 6.2–6.5; G2, Appendix; F2]

**Stem selection limit.** Select regular stems whose form remains stable before vowel-initial suffixes. Turkish has lexeme-specific stem alternations (for example `kitap` → `kitab-` before a vowel, while other final voiceless consonants resist this), so a universal “voice every final p/t/ç/k” rule would be inaccurate. Either leave such stems out of the first sample or list a second stem allomorph for each selected lexeme and cite it individually. Avoid exceptional loanword harmony and vowel-deleting stems unless they are deliberately added with their own evidence. [G1, §5.2; G3, chs. 2, 5]

## 4. Plausible planted modelling mistakes

These are **proposed** mistakes in a FieldWorks project, with predicted diagnostic reasoning rather than quotations of PanGloss trace output. The future builder must pin the actual affected word list and parser results. FieldWorks exposes slot order, slot optionality, allomorph environments, active templates, and parse tracing, so each is an authorable error. [F1–F5]

| Mistake in the broken variant | Example forms it should affect | Predicted parser reason and lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restrict plural `-ler` to only a subclass such as `e/i` instead of **all front vowels**, omitting `ö/ü`. | `gözler` (`göz-ler`, eye-PL), `güller` (`gül-ler`, rose-PL). | The surface suffix `-ler` is found, but its allomorph environment rejects a stem whose last vowel is `ö` or `ü`; `-lar` has the wrong surface vowel. The trace should end at plural allomorph selection. This teaches a natural-class correction. [G1, §6.3; G2, Appendix; F2] |
| Move the possessive slot before the plural slot. | `evlerim` (`ev-ler-im`, house-PL-POSS.1SG), `gözlerim` (`göz-ler-im`, eye-PL-POSS.1SG). | The template permits `POSS.1SG` before `PL` but the target needs `PL` before `POSS.1SG`; no legal slot path consumes the morphemes in their observed order. The trace should identify the template/slot dead end. [G1, §6.2; F1] |
| Enter only consonant-final DAT/ACC allomorphs and omit the **y** forms. | `masaya` (`masa-ya`, table-DAT), `kediyi` (`kedi-yi`, cat-ACC), `kutuyu` (`kutu-yu`, box-ACC). | After a vowel-final noun, no case allomorph accounts for the surface **y**; the parser cannot consume the whole word. The remedy is a vowel-final conditioned case allomorph, not a new stem. [G1, §§6.5.3–6.5.4; G2, Appendix; F2] |
| **Speed bug:** while experimenting, duplicate the Noun affix template repeatedly and leave twelve equivalent copies active on the same Noun category. | The whole noun Selection, especially long forms such as `evlerimde` (`ev-ler-im-de`) and `gözlerimden` (`göz-ler-im-den`). Correct parses should still be possible. | FieldWorks can duplicate templates and tries applicable active templates for a stem. Twelve equivalent paths therefore make the parser revisit essentially the same slot sequence many times; this predicts more work without a coverage loss. The copy count is a tunable proposed fixture, **not evidence of a twelvefold PanGloss cost**: record a machine-independent attempt/work measure and adjust the fixture until the gated real-parser test demonstrates the plan's ≥10× target. The first three mistakes have disjoint intended lessons; overlap in an actual broken project may change which failure a parser reports first. Pin each bug against its own target words, and keep at least one unaffected comparison word for each rule. The slowness claim is a **prediction from template multiplicity**, not a measurement; publish a numeric speed claim only after PanGloss v0.5.0 supplies the work ratio. [P1; F4–F6]

## 5. Recommended teaching simplifications

Every item below is a **departure from real Turkish** for the synthetic project.

1. **Departure:** Model nouns only, with plural, first-person singular possession, and five overt cases (ACC, DAT, LOC, ABL, GEN). Real Turkish has more possessive persons, additional case constructions, derivation, and productive verb morphology. The narrower template keeps every contrast tied to the two teaching goals. [G1, chs. 6, 15–30; G2, Appendix]
2. **Departure:** Treat the bare stem as the sample's “singular/nominative” teaching label and omit syntactic rules for bare direct objects, quantified nouns, possessive constructions, and differential object marking. Real Turkish bare forms are not simply English singular nouns, and ACC is conditioned by discourse/syntax. Do not claim these labels exhaust Turkish meanings. [G1, §§6.3, 6.5.1, 6.5.4; G2, “Accusative case”]
3. **Departure:** Use only stems with regular suffix harmony and stable consonants in the core paradigm; exclude proper names, exceptional borrowings, vowel-deleting nouns, and unpredictable stem alternations. Turkish has all of these. Add a specific stem allomorph only if the sourced data calls for it and the lesson needs it. [G1, §§5.1–5.2; G3, ch. 2]
4. **Departure:** Encode harmony and buffer consonants as visible, conditioned suffix allomorphs rather than a full Turkish phonology. This is a modelling choice for teaching FieldWorks allomorph environments, not a claim that speakers store every listed form separately. [G1, §§5.1, 5.3; F2]
5. **Departure:** Do not model Turkish's emphatic partial reduplication of adjectives and adverbs. No infix position is needed for the selected noun paradigm; the site plan teaches infix authoring in its separate synthetic Philippine-style sample. [G4; P1, §4]
