# Stems and the lexicon

The lexicon is the grammar’s store of words and their linguistic information. A lexical entry may include a stem, a gloss that reminds you of its meaning, and a part of speech such as noun or verb. Those labels help the grammar decide which forms and patterns can apply.

As an illustrative English example, *walk* is the stem, *to move on foot* is a possible gloss, and *verb* is its part of speech. The form *walked* can be built from that stem and a past-tense ending. A gloss is a short guide for people, not a complete definition or a promise that two languages divide meaning in the same way.

One of the most common causes of failure is a missing stem. If a grammar expects to start with *walk* but that stem is absent or entered under a different form, adding the ending rule will not give the parser a starting point. In a simplified, illustrative Turkish example, the stem *ev* (“house”) is needed before the grammar can analyse *evler* (“houses”) with the plural suffix.

When a word fails, compare the form you typed with the lexicon entry you expect. Check spelling and the relevant writing system, then check that the entry has the stem and part of speech your analysis requires. If the stem is there, move on to the affixes and rules that should build the rest of the word.

The examples here are illustrative; real entries and analyses depend on the language and the choices made in its FieldWorks project.
